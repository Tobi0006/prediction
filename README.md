## 📖 About the Book

**From Open Intelligence to Prediction** is a comprehensive educational and technical guide exploring how Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT), statistical forecasting, artificial intelligence, and Transformer architectures can be integrated into reliable, evidence-based prediction workflows.

The book bridges three essential disciplines:

- **Intelligence Analysis:** Collecting, verifying, interpreting, and evaluating open-source information.
- **AI Engineering:** Building reproducible data pipelines and Transformer-based forecasting models.
- **Predictive Research:** Evaluating forecast accuracy, uncertainty, model reliability, and decision-making.

Using pandemic intelligence as its primary application, the book introduces an end-to-end methodology for transforming publicly available information into structured datasets, forecasting future outcomes, and producing transparent intelligence reports.

It progresses from fundamental analytical concepts to advanced machine learning architectures, including Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), PatchTST, and iTransformer.

Rather than focusing solely on predictive accuracy, the book emphasizes **reproducibility, source credibility, uncertainty quantification, ethical intelligence practices, and human oversight**.

**PatchTST**
- Time-series patching
- Long-context learning
- Channel-independent representations

## 🌟 Key Principles

The book emphasizes several fundamental ideas:

**Evidence before prediction.** Reliable forecasts begin with trustworthy observations, clearly defined targets, and documented sources.

**Validation before complexity.** Advanced Transformer architectures should demonstrate measurable improvement over simpler forecasting methods.

**Uncertainty is essential.** A useful forecast communicates possible outcomes and limitations rather than presenting a single number as certainty.

**Reproducibility builds trust.** Forecasts should be traceable to their original data, model versions, evaluation methods, and publication dates.

**Human judgment remains necessary.** AI systems support intelligence analysis and decision-making but do not replace independent verification, expert review, or responsible oversight.
